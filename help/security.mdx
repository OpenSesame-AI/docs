Security is the foundation of Magic. Your private key is protected by hardware-level security built into your device and is never exposed to Magic's servers. Understanding how this works — and what you can do on your end — gives you the strongest possible protection for your assets.

## How Magic Protects Your Keys

When you create a Magic wallet, your private key is generated inside a protected area built directly into your device's hardware — think of it as a locked safe that exists only on your device. Nothing running outside that safe can read what's inside, even if the rest of the device were somehow compromised.

Here's what that means in practice:

- **Magic never sees your private key.** Not during sign-in, not during transactions, not ever — not even Magic employees have access to it.
- **Each sign-in is verified without storing a password.** When you sign in, Magic confirms your identity using a one-time code or your social account, so there is no password database that could ever be stolen.
- **Your key stays on your device.** It is never uploaded to Magic's servers at any point.

<Note>
  Magic is non-custodial — this means even if Magic's servers were ever breached, your private key remains protected inside your device's hardware and is never at risk from a server-side attack.
</Note>

## What You Can Do to Stay Safe

Strong security is a partnership. Magic handles the infrastructure — here's how you protect your side.

## What to Do If Your Email Is Compromised

If you believe someone else has gained access to your email account, act quickly:

1. **Change your email password immediately** and enable two-factor authentication (2FA) if you haven't already.
2. **Sign in to Magic** and go to **Settings → Security** to revoke all connected apps and active sessions.
3. **Add a passkey or a different social login** (such as Apple or Google) as your primary sign-in method so you're no longer dependent on that email address.
4. **Export your private key** from **Settings → Security** and move your assets to a new wallet address if you believe your account was accessed by someone else.
5. **Contact Magic support** at [support@magic.link](mailto:support@magic.link) so our team can help you secure your account.

## Recognizing Phishing and Scams

Attackers often impersonate legitimate services to steal credentials or private keys. Keep the following in mind:

- Magic will **never** ask for your private key by email, chat, or any other channel.
- Always check the web address before signing in — it should be **magic.link**. Look for the padlock icon and confirm the domain exactly.
- Be suspicious of requests to approve transactions for unusually large amounts, especially if they arrive unexpectedly.
- Never click "Connect Wallet" links sent to you via direct messages, emails, or social media — always navigate to the app directly.

<Warning>
  If you suspect your account has been compromised, immediately go to **Settings → Security** and revoke all connected apps and active sessions. Then contact [Magic support](/help/contact) so we can assist you.
</Warning>
