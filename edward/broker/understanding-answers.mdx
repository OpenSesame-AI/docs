Every Edward answer is designed to be **checkable**. You never have to take its word for it; you can trace any answer back to the exact carrier rule it came from.

## Citations

Edward cites the source behind each answer. Click a citation to open the original document and see the rule in context, the same carrier manual or bulletin you'd cite yourself.

<Tip>
  When an answer will go to a client or underwriter, open the citation and confirm the rule reads the way Edward summarized it. The source is always one click away.
</Tip>

## Confidence levels

Edward tells you how confident it is in each answer:

| Level | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| **High** | The answer is well supported by authoritative carrier documents. |
| **Medium** | The answer is supported, but with less certainty, so it's worth a quick check against the citation. |
| **Low** | Edward found limited or weaker evidence. Verify before you rely on it. |

Use the confidence level as a cue for how much to double-check. A high-confidence, well-cited answer is safe to act on; a low-confidence one deserves a look at the source.

## When Edward is unsure

Edward is built to be honest about what it doesn't know:

- **No document found:** if nothing in your library covers the question, Edward tells you plainly instead of inventing an answer. If you think a document *should* exist, ask your admin whether it's been uploaded and verified.
- **General answer only:** if Edward can only answer from general background rather than an authoritative carrier document, it flags that the answer isn't grounded in a specific underwriting rule.

<Warning>
  If an answer isn't cited to a carrier document, treat it as general guidance, not a confirmed underwriting rule.
</Warning>

## How Edward handles conflicting documents

When two documents disagree (say, a manual and a newer bulletin), Edward follows the rule that **the newer authority governs**. It uses the most recent applicable document and layers newer bulletins on top of the base manual, so you get the current rule rather than an outdated one.

<Info>
  If you spot an answer that seems to cite an outdated rule, let your admin know; it may mean a newer bulletin or manual edition still needs to be uploaded.
</Info>
