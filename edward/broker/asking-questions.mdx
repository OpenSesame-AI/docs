You don't need special syntax or keywords. Just ask Edward the way you'd ask a knowledgeable colleague. A few habits will get you sharper, faster answers.

<Frame caption="Open Ask Ed and type your question in the box. No special syntax needed.">

</Frame>

## Ask in plain language

Ask real underwriting questions in natural language. For example:

- "Will this carrier write a restaurant with a deep fryer?"
- "Can I write a DB-12 Aston Martin, and which carrier is the better fit?"
- "What's the minimum deductible for a wood-frame building in this region?"

Edward decides whether your question needs underwriting evidence and pulls the relevant carrier rules to answer it.

## Set the carrier, product, and region

Edward answers within a scope: a specific **carrier**, **product**, and **region**. It works this out for you:

- If you name a carrier or region in your question, Edward uses it.
- If you don't, Edward falls back to your **default region** and your assigned scopes.

<Note>
  If your question is missing a detail Edward needs, or several carriers could match, it will ask you to confirm before answering rather than guess. Answering that prompt takes a second and keeps the answer accurate.
</Note>

## Ask follow-up questions

Edward remembers the context of your conversation. Once you've established the carrier and product, you can keep drilling in without repeating yourself:

> "Will Carrier X write a bakery?"
> "What about with a wood-fired oven?"
> "And if the building is over 40 years old?"

Each follow-up builds on the last, so you get a natural back-and-forth instead of starting over each time.

## Reference a specific document

If you want an answer from a particular document, mention or attach it in your question. Edward will focus on that document rather than searching your whole library.

<Tip>
  Include the details that matter to underwriting: property type, construction, location, size, and any risk features (like a deep fryer or a pool). The more specific your question, the more precise the rule Edward can cite.
</Tip>
